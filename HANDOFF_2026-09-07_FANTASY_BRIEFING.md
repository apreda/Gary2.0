## What changed

`mlbFantasyEvidence.js` collects complete lineups, distinguishes posted and prior orders, preserves doubleheaders, joins players exactly, and connects recent games, season measurements, Savant contact quality, opposing pitchers and the measured opposing batting order. Missing plate appearances never become at-bats or a fabricated strikeout rate. Innings and recent ERA/WHIP/K-per-nine are derived from complete measured outs, using baseball innings notation. Existing published injury reports are read verbatim as conflict context; their source logic, labels and duration handling were not changed.

`nflFantasyProvider.js` and `nflFantasyEvidence.js` use the existing BALLDONTLIE connection. The final live collection resolved all 272 regular-season games and the actual Wednesday-opening Week 1, September 9–14. It read 427 forecasts and 944 ownership rows, selecting 32 candidates across four positions, including nine first-season listings. Twenty-three dated prior-season baselines were available. All 25 gated requests succeeded. No current-season regular game observations existed for these players on September 7; the writer explicitly treats prior samples as baselines and weekly data as forecasts.

`fantasyDecision.js` lets the model decide which calls merit publication; no statistical threshold assigns an add or tier. It validates complete JSON, identities, evidence references, formats, full text, and cited numbers. Cross-player comparisons explicitly cite and label the other player's evidence. Next-game comparisons close at the earliest referenced player's kickoff or first pitch, while retaining the subject's own matchup for display. A forecast is not a scoring ceiling or floor. Game start does not imply a user's league waiver or lineup-lock policy.

The prompt compacts repeated scalar row keys losslessly and rejects oversize evidence instead of clipping it. A validation retry receives all detected errors and the full evidence for selected players and their cited peers, rather than resending the entire discovery pool. The reviewed MLB generation passed on its first attempt with a 190,691-byte prompt. NFL used 186,515 bytes initially and 99,411 bytes for its one correction, about 47% less input for the correction. Both used the existing Codex Sol bridge; no new vendor or paid hosting was added. The generation counters record prompt bytes, total prompt bytes, attempts, elapsed time and decision count.

The independent Mac worker uses a kernel lock that releases after a process dies, bounded execution, cancellation, source-based expiry and unchanged-input reuse. Reuse never refreshes old source timestamps. One guarded database transaction updates the briefing and older-app projection, preventing partial publication or slower old work overwriting a newer collection. Public clients have read access; only the service role may publish. General insight resets preserve either Fantasy provenance marker, and football grading treats these roster decisions as context rather than team win/loss predictions. Old noninjury MLB/NFL Fantasy computers were removed from the daily registry so they cannot reintroduce duplicate or contradictory calls.

Native clients separate categories/points for MLB and Standard/Half PPR/PPR for NFL, and offer All calls, Pickups and Lineup filters. Full explanations and sources remain readable. Valid NFL advice can carry across midnight; a temporary refresh failure preserves that still-current briefing. Lists and open detail sheets enforce expiry. Player links require a populated card for the exact league, player, day and game; otherwise they open the full Fantasy decision instead of an empty or wrong-game card.
