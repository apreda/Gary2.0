## Round 1: five adult usability sessions

Recruit five willing people aged 21+ who already follow sports and have kept track of picks or bets. Use the existing company audience or people Adam knows who explicitly volunteer. Do not buy a gambling promotion or send unsolicited automated messages. Let volunteers decline analytics and still participate. Do not collect birthdays, sportsbook credentials, balances, full betting histories or recordings without a separate need and consent.

Allow 15 minutes each. Use an existing bet or a clearly fictional example. Nobody needs to place a new wager, increase a stake or spend money to test Gary. Any test entry is private; remove it afterward if unwanted. Exclude internal usability examples from claims about organic adoption.

1. Start from the proposed social link. Without coaching, find the game you care about and explain where Gary's reasoning is. If today's pick is not published, find a dated example and explain that it is historical.
2. Visit Your Book. Ask what they expect it to do before signing in. Record their words rather than explaining the feature first.
3. After signing in, log the example using their chosen units and odds. Time from the usable Book to a saved entry; exclude sign-in time and record sign-in friction separately.
4. Settle the example, correct the result, find it with a filter, and export or delete it. Ask who can see the entry and whether Gary placed a wager or connected to a sportsbook.
5. Ask them to distinguish the private manual record, automatically settled tail/fade choices and Gary's own game-pick results. Do not suggest these are comparable samples without the same dates and market scope.

Proposed usability gate: at least four of five can save and settle an entry without coaching, save within two minutes after sign-in, and correctly explain privacy and the record labels. A gate is an internal decision rule, not statistical proof. Any loss of data, privacy misunderstanding or incorrect settlement blocks expansion until fixed and retested with a fresh volunteer.

## Round 2: ten-person, seven-day usefulness pilot

After the usability fixes pass, invite up to ten willing adults who say tracking is a real need. Start each observation window at their first successful manual save. Tell them to return only if useful; do not manufacture activity with repeated prompts. One optional feedback conversation after the full seven-day window is enough.

Ask: What made you return? What stopped you? What do you use today? Which entry step takes too much effort? Would automatic import solve a problem you actually encountered? Which sportsbook would you want supported? A request for import is not a willingness to pay or authorization to connect an account.

Proposed learning gate: at least five of ten return to review or update their Book on a later day, with at least three describing a concrete recurring benefit in their own words. Count both the numerator and all enrolled participants; report withdrawals and missing observations separately. This small convenience sample guides the next build and does not prove market fit. If the gate misses, fix the most common problem and run another bounded round before paying for import infrastructure.

Website telemetry is a secondary, consented-browser measure. `npm run report:funnel -- --week YYYY-MM-DD` reports unique sessions with manual saves/settlements and return to a successfully loaded Book 24 hours to less than seven days after first observed manual save. Only mature seven-day windows enter the return percentage. Session refreshes do not count as opens. Declined consent, device changes, blocked requests and late consent produce missing observations; do not turn missing telemetry into a participant failure.
