THE WINNERS REVIEW — the questions. Plain yes or no, one line of evidence each, quoted from the case or the card.

PART 1 — THE PICKED SIDE'S CASE
1. Recent, not the season. Are the case's main claims about what has happened lately — this quarterback's and this offense's last 3 to 4 games, this defense's last 3 to 4 games, this week's injury report and who actually practiced? Or last season, preseason, career numbers, "the better roster"? Count both kinds of claim. Yes only when recent work carries the case.
2. A this-week reason. Does the case name a specific reason this side covers or wins this week — this passing game against that secondary, this run game against that front, a specific absence, this offensive line against that pass rush — rather than "the better team"?
3. The other side answered. Does the case, or the card, deal with the other case's strongest point?

PART 2 — THE OTHER SIDE'S CASE
Questions 1 and 2 again, for the other side, the same way.

PART 3 — THE COMPARISON
4. Which case is stronger: the picked side, the other side, or even? Separately: are both cases weak — both teams with real doubts, no reason to pick a side this week?

PART 4 — THE CARD (what the paid reader sees)
5. Does the card carry the case's actual reasons, or a paraphrase that drops the story?
6. Do the reasons fit the bet? Moneyline: reasons about who wins. Laying points: a reason this team wins by more than the number. Taking points: a reason it stays inside the number or wins. A close-game story under a big lay is a no.

PART 5 — OUTSIDE THE DESK (use web search here)
7. This week's news. Anything from the last 24 hours the desk didn't have that cuts against the pick — a late scratch, a quarterback change, a starter ruled out or downgraded, weather? Name the item and the source.
8. Your own read after looking around, two lines: serious doubts about the picked side, or about both teams?
