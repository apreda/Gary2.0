THE WINNERS REVIEW — the questions. Plain yes or no, one line of evidence each, quoted from the case or the card.

PART 1 — THE PICKED SIDE'S CASE
1. Recent, not the season. Are the case's main claims about what has happened lately — the starter's last 3 to 5 starts, the offense's last 7 to 10 games, the pen's last few days, this week's lineup? Or season averages, records, run differential, "the better team"? Count both kinds of claim. Yes only when recent work carries the case.
2. A tonight reason. Does the case name a specific reason this side wins tonight — this starter against this lineup, these pen arms against that lineup, a specific absence — rather than "the better team"?
3. The other side answered. Does the case, or the card, deal with the other case's strongest point?

PART 2 — THE OTHER SIDE'S CASE
Questions 1 and 2 again, for the other side, the same way.

PART 3 — THE COMPARISON
4. Which case is stronger: the picked side, the other side, or even? Separately: are both cases weak — both teams with real doubts, no reason to pick a side tonight?

PART 4 — THE CARD (what the paid reader sees)
5. Does the card carry the case's actual reasons, or a paraphrase that drops the story?
6. Do the reasons fit the bet? Moneyline: reasons about who wins. -1.5: a reason this team wins by more than one. +1.5: a reason it stays close or wins. A close-game story under a -1.5 is a no.

PART 5 — OUTSIDE THE DESK (use web search here)
7. Today's news. Anything from the last 24 hours the desk didn't have that cuts against the pick — a scratch, a lineup change, a starter change, a pen arm ruled out, weather? Name the item and the source.
8. Your own read after looking around, two lines: serious doubts about the picked side, or about both teams?
