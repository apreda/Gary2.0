THE WINNERS REVIEW — exact-ticket-v2. Answer yes, no or unknown, with specific evidence and its source. Unknown cannot qualify. This review checks the quality and support of an existing decision; it does not establish a profitable betting edge.

PART 1 — EACH SIDE'S CASE, BEFORE LEARNING THE BET
1. Evidence in context. Are the decisive claims supported by the supplied facts with the right dates, season, sample and present matchup/role context? Recent evidence and season evidence can both be useful. Neither receives automatic preference. Repeated news is not additional independent evidence.
2. Matchup reason. Does this case give a specific, supported account of this matchup? Identify its strongest supported point. Separate facts from the case writer's interpretation.
3. Opposing evidence. Does this case address the opposing case's strongest supported point, and how?

PART 2 — THE OTHER SIDE'S CASE
Apply the same three questions to the other side. Neither favorite status nor underdog status changes the standard.
For baseball, keep starter season and recent-start samples distinct; identify the lineup/opponent, innings or plate appearances, and the dates behind workload claims. A recent hot streak is not automatically stronger evidence than the season.

PART 3 — THE COMPARISON
Which argument is better supported, or are they even? Are both weak? Record this as context. Before seeing the exact ticket and price, this comparison cannot determine admission. A less likely winner can still be a defensible ticket at its price.

PART 4 — THE EXACT TICKET AND CARD
Does the card preserve the material reasons and qualifications in the decision?
Are the decisive facts supported by identifiable supplied evidence or verified sources, with their dates and sample context?
Do those reasons address this moneyline, the exact signed spread/run line, or this player's over/under at the exact stat line?
Does the existing reasoning engage with the actual offered price? Likely winner, attractive payout, confidence or a bare assertion of value alone is insufficient. Do not invent a probability, demand a numerical edge, or improve Gary's argument on his behalf.
Does the decision answer the strongest supported opposing evidence for this exact ticket?
Is a central assumption or contradiction unresolved — something the decision relies on that could change the ticket? Distinguish that from ordinary uncertainty about a future result.
For props, inspect the original prop source desk and card; a separate two-club debate is not required. Identify the opposing point in the supplied evidence, without inventing one.

PART 5 — AVAILABLE NEWS
Check dated sources for relevant information available before the review and kickoff. Do not use game results, in-game observations or later reporting. Was the news check completed? Does material news contradict a decisive premise that the decision leaves unaddressed? Cite the source URL and publication date. If search or source timing cannot be verified, answer unknown. Already-considered unfavorable news does not automatically invalidate a decision.
